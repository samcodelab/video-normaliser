Colour correction helps the flashes, but the regional model remains incomplete

This round added native RGB measurements, source-selected persistent RGB diagnostics and an experimental colour-continuity refinement. No candidate earned promotion. The app’s correction defaults remain unchanged, and the main LEGO flash remains unresolved.

What the RGB measurements establish

Fox’s source flashes include substantial colour changes. At 558 → 559, median regional channel changes are approximately −1.00 EV red, −0.93 green and −1.37 blue. The existing correction reduces those to −0.064, −0.032 and −0.199 EV. Blue therefore retains a larger residual than the other channels.

Removing the final field’s colour component increases median chromatic step from 0.074 to 0.178 EV on that transition. This counterfactual is not a complete slider refit, but shows why simply reducing Colour does not resolve the main flash. In contrast, at the nearly unchanged source transition 559 → 560, the correction introduces a smaller colour echo: median chromatic step is 0.0098 EV, versus a source step around 0.00007 EV.

Persistent source evidence

Source-only tracking and held/interior RGB response checks qualify seven sampled footprints at Fox frame 169, six at 349 and five at 559. These are sparse thumbnail measurements; overlapping tracks are not independent donors, and uniform RGB response alone does not establish physical lighting truth.

LEGO frame 188 qualifies zero sampled footprints in both the scene-start episode and the later 184–192 episode. In the latter, 47 of 66 seeds lack persistent geometry, ten encounter dark/clipped channels and nine fail uniform held RGB response. This explains why extending a simple exposure model into those regions is unproven; it does not establish that no useful source evidence exists at any resolution or sampling position.

The implementation tested

The prototype stabilises rendered chroma in source-quiet footprints, keeps their linear luminance fixed, and limits each added channel gain to 0.05 × Strength × Spatial × Colour EV. It checks actual guided/highlight rendering, preserves per-edge chromatic quality and requires aggregate improvement. It never warps or blends source images. The final guarded version also requires a corroborated colour event, disjoint donors, wide source identity and a temporal span bounded by the existing radius. Reference-area and incompatible/row-model paths abstain.

The first correction-gain average was rejected by its own guards. The revised rendered-colour target was active, but independent exported-control checks found mixed results. The final event-conditioned implementation passes a positive synthetic contract and abstains on these real videos and controls. That abstention is not an improvement claim.

Decision and next model requirement

Keep the refinement default-off. It addresses a small correction-induced colour echo, not the main source lighting error. Further solver conditioning or weaker source gates would not establish the missing evidence. The next model needs source-qualified regional RGB response through the existing residual reconstruction, with persistent correspondence and explicit treatment of mixed illumination, highlights and uncertainty. A scene-wide RGB gain cannot be assumed to fit differently lit materials.

Strength, Spatial, Colour and radius remain useful controls. Reducing Colour can reduce a small correction-induced echo while worsening a genuine coloured flash elsewhere, so slider guidance must explain that trade-off. For LEGO, the investigated coverage/model gap remains an algorithm issue. No new UI mode or slider was added.
