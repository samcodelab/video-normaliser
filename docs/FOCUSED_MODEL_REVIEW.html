Where correction succeeds, and where the model runs out of evidence

New attribution diagnostics cover all 212 LEGO frames and 600 Fox frames from the retained V62 experiment. No correction algorithm or application defaults changed. Results distinguish global gain, local-field contribution and brightness-anchor/validation contribution.

Method and boundaries

The runner uses the frozen V62 renderer and retained source thumbnails and fields. It measures 24 × 14 fixed cells for source, global-only, local-without-anchor and complete correction. Ordered contributions telescope exactly per cell, including nonlinear highlight limiting. This is a counterfactual decomposition of an existing field, not an independent refit of each stage. The affine path’s anchor removal also removes its conditional tone limiting.

These are thumbnail predictions, not full-resolution encoded measurements or motion-compensated clean-light truth. Motion, reflectance and lighting all affect fixed cells. Scene-cut transitions are excluded from event ranking. High applied gain is not proof of overcorrection. The investigated user-export file is not identified by these retained benchmarks.

This is distinct from the previously reported 0.119 EV matched-surface jump: different sampling and statistics mean the numbers are not interchangeable. The large event at 157 → 158 requires visual and motion/scene verification; it is a candidate global-target problem, not a confirmed defect.

Fox’s ranked events mostly show strong source darkening with substantial global compensation and smaller local/anchor contributions. They suggest remaining amplitude/region errors rather than a universal positive gain spike. Frame 307 has median total applied gain +0.748 EV, consisting of median global +0.617, local +0.099 and anchor +0.032. Its source darkens from frame 306, so the gain alone does not establish excessive brightness.

Model decision

Stop prioritising additional solver iterations. Retain the current general correction path while investigating separate global-target errors and local coverage failures. Establish a shot brightness target from persistent representative surfaces, with deliberate lighting trends preserved in Smooth mode and an explicit stable target in Steady mode. Local residual fields need support boundaries and independent quiet-region protection; unknown areas cannot be treated as known quiet illumination.

The research supports reviewing persistent temporal representation and illumination evidence rather than only numerical optimisation. BlazeBVD uses illumination priors and global/local correction; neural-atlas deflickering addresses temporal consistency and unreliable correspondence; intrinsic-video research identifies illumination/reflectance ambiguity. These are methodological leads, not proof that their models solve our footage. Commercial tools have not been benchmarked against these clips.

Next decisive checks

  1. Verify the selected events at native resolution, using identical source/output playback and actual gain maps. Include LEGO 157–166 and 186–190, Fox 168–170, 305–308, 348–350 and 558–560.
  2. Separate stationary surfaces from motion and reject near-black/clipped cells before interpreting event peaks. Record qualified, rejected and unknown support per region.
  3. Test the global scene target and persistent local model separately. Require lower peak residuals and preserved brightness/quiet regions, not merely a lower fit-row objective.
  4. Validate partial Strength/Spatial/Colour and temporal-radius settings against independent targets and untouched clean-motion/intentional-light controls before promotion.
