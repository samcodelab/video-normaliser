What is usable now

  • Scene-specific Smooth/Steady correction, Strength, Spatial, Colour and radius controls.
  • Manual ±2 EV edits on an individual source frame, saved in projects, with timeline markers.
  • Large nearby-frame comparison in the main preview for checking individual frame adjustments.
  • Sliders with explanations of when to reduce overall, local or colour correction.
  • Fox scene detection now finds the two known cuts at frames 200 and 400; the earlier implementation missed both.

The interface and detection work is earlier thread context, not a new result of the last experimental fits. Recent experimental flags have not been promoted into the app’s normal correction behavior.

What remains unresolved

  • LEGO’s remaining regional flashes and inconsistent local correction.
  • Fixing a flickering background without worsening a moving foreground.
  • Some clipped/highlight and intentional-lighting trade-offs.
  • Reliable behavior across unfamiliar production videos.
  • Evidence that the flashes are no longer visible during playback.

Rendering changes gains on original source pixels. It does not synthesize or warp neighboring frames. That helps avoid geometric morphing, but uneven gains can still cause visible patches, halos or changing apparent contours.

The latest experiment is not the best result

V66’s tighter solve gives LEGO overall variation of 0.03531 EV and a main jump of 0.12521 EV, worse than V62’s 0.03515 / 0.11877 EV. Against the V45 motion-control comparator, V66 reduces background flicker by about 2.4% but increases foreground flicker by about 0.5%; its background high-error tail also worsens. This is a mixed result, not a release candidate.

Some main fits still hit the 2,000-iteration limit. More accurate arithmetic alone has not delivered a useful LEGO improvement. The newest source-only V67 conditioning change passes five focused tests but has no encoded-video validation. It remains disabled by default.

Historical V66 logs labelled maximum iterate change as “dualResidual.” V67 separates that quantity from the proper ADMM dual residual. Satisfying constraints, reaching numerical convergence and improving visible quality are three different checks.

Half Strength helped the synthetic motion control in V63, but it did not solve LEGO: its important jump grew from roughly 0.12 EV at full Strength to roughly 0.21 EV at half Strength. A favorable setting on one clip cannot be transferred automatically to another.

Evidence is stronger than at the beginning

We now have clean-reference synthetic clips, separate foreground/background masks, matched real-video transitions, partial-slider targets, exact media checks and frozen source/binary/environment records. Tests cover motion, cuts, flashes, fades, colour changes, highlights and altered materials. The CC0 clay animation adds a legally usable external test case.

The promoted confidence revision was historically validated with 144 native tests and 450 encoded slider cases. V45 has broader 26-case motion/adversarial evidence, with preserved timing/cuts and unchanged clean-motion scores, but some foreground/background and highlight regressions. The newest helper changes have focused tests; those historical totals are not a fresh full-suite pass for today’s entire worktree.

Commercial tools have not been run against these same clips, so we cannot claim parity with their advertised results. Several test cases were used during tuning; they are regression evidence, not all untouched holdouts.

iPhone and iPad direction

The architecture review recommends a shared processing/session core with separate macOS and adaptive iPhone/iPad interfaces. Most processing logic is shareable. Mobile memory, coordinated file access, interrupted export and checkpoint-preserving suspension need work first. Retained analysis can reach roughly 1.05 GB per minute at 30 fps when both optional detail rasters are enabled. No iOS target or device-tested build exists yet.

My assessment and the next decision

The app and validation process are better. The recent experimental round has yielded more diagnosis than usable visual improvement.

I am not satisfied with LEGO, and I would not describe FrankLuma as reliably fixing most videos yet. The full-strength experimental fits remain disabled. We should resume from a clearly identified baseline, not from whichever candidate happened to run last.

  1. Review short, identical playback loops of source, retained baseline and the strongest candidate, agreeing which visible defects actually count as failures.
  2. Choose one remaining failure to investigate, with moving-subject and intentional-lighting controls beside it; avoid further unfocused parameter changes.
  3. Promote only a candidate that improves those visible failures across multiple clips, preserves foregrounds and intended lighting, and has fresh broad and partial-slider validation.

Work is paused. No further implementation or evaluation jobs are being launched.
